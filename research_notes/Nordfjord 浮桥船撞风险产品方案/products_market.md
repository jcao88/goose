## Q1. Risk-assessment tools: who makes them, how they model, licensing, strengths and weaknesses

### Takeaway
The design-phase market rests on a few probabilistic tools that are decades old: IWRAP Mk2 (IALA/GateHouse, cheap per-user licence), MARIN SAMSON (owned by the Dutch Ministry), DNV MARCS, Dovre Safetec COLLIDE (Norwegian Continental Shelf, NCS), Anatec COLLRISK/ShipRoutes (UK offshore wind). All are "static traffic × causation factor" models, and none couples to structural physics. Ship-handling simulators (FORCE SimFlex, BMT REMBRANDT, Kongsberg-based) cover the deterministic side. The closest thing to a "physics + Monte Carlo" product is academic: Chalmers' STAPS / STAPS-cons, which has already been applied to Bjørnafjorden.

### Cited Findings

**IALA IWRAP Mk2 (developed and sold by GateHouse A/S for IALA)**
- Theory and modelling: IWRAP is a probabilistic tool that estimates collision, allision and grounding frequencies for shipping routes. It rests on the Fujii, MacDuff, Pedersen and Friis-Hansen work from the 1970s–1990s, the same lineage behind the Eurocode bridge-impact provisions — [ESREL 2023 paper](https://www.rpsonline.com.sg/proceedings/esrel2023/html/P183.html); [IWRAP theory document](https://www.iala.int/wiki/iwrap/images/2/2b/IWRAP_Theory.pdf)
- Maintainer and versions: GateHouse A/S authors the manuals (Per Christian Engberg): v4.7.0 (Nov 2015) and v5.3.0 (Sep 2017). The first commercial version of IWRAP Mk2 came out on 3 Aug 2010 — [Manual v4.7.0](https://www.iala.int/wiki/iwrap/images/d/d0/Iwrapmk2.pdf); [Manual v5.3.0](https://www.iala.int/wiki/iwrap/images/7/75/IWRAP_Mk2_Manual_v5.3.pdf); [IALA wiki – Commercial version](https://www.iala.int/wiki/iwrap/index.php/Commercial_version)
- Licence model: a "freemium" model. The free Basic version is open to IALA members only: the user generates a key request, emails it to IALA, IALA checks membership and returns the licence key. Anyone can buy the commercial variant — [IWRAP FAQ](https://www.iala.int/wiki/iwrap/index.php/IWRAP_FAQ); [GateHouse 2017 training deck](https://www.iala.int/wiki/iwrap/images/5/51/IWRAP_Mk2_GH_2017.pdf). The 2017 deck seems to say "Basic version free to use for all", which conflicts with the FAQ's members-only rule. Treat this as unresolved.
- Commercial pricing (undated wiki page, probably stale): IALA members pay €3,500 in year 1 and €2,800 a year after. Non-members pay €6,000 in year 1 and €4,500 a year after. Licences are per user, and the contact is iwrap@gatehouse.dk — [IALA wiki – Commercial version](https://www.iala.int/wiki/iwrap/index.php/Commercial_version). The 2017 GateHouse deck quotes €2,900 per user for a one-year licence, with later years cheaper — [GateHouse 2017 deck](https://www.iala.int/wiki/iwrap/images/5/51/IWRAP_Mk2_GH_2017.pdf)
- Commercial vs Basic features: only the commercial version imports AIS automatically. It also generates density maps, extracts lateral distributions automatically and fits mathematical distributions to them. The Basic version has no automatic AIS import — [IALA wiki – Commercial version](https://www.iala.int/wiki/iwrap/index.php/Commercial_version); [GateHouse 2017 deck](https://www.iala.int/wiki/iwrap/images/5/51/IWRAP_Mk2_GH_2017.pdf)
- XML and automation: the Danish Maritime Safety Administration (DaMSA) built tools *outside* IWRAP that process AIS and compile it into an IWRAP waterway model file (XML). No source describes built-in batch or scripting capability — [IALA wiki – Processing AIS Data](https://www.iala.int/wiki/iwrap/index.php/Processing_AIS_Data)
- Training ecosystem: IALA sells a course, "Risk Assessment using the IALA IWRAP Mk2 tool", for €1,000, and runs L1.3 courses on the IALA risk management toolbox (e.g., Suriname, Wenzhou) — [IALA shop](https://www.iala.int/shop/page/20); [IALA L1.3 Suriname](https://iala.int/content/uploads/2024/11/L1.3-Use-of-IALA-Risk-Management-toolboxe-Suriname.pdf); [IALA L1.3 Wenzhou](https://www.iala.int/?p=103997)
- Accuracy critique: a 2025 Reliability Engineering & System Safety paper (Chalmers/RISE) found IWRAP Mk2 predicts about 4.5× higher allision probability than the STAPS mid-fidelity simulator in the base case. Failure duration and route layout strongly influence both models. The authors recommend IWRAP for early design and STAPS for later stages — [Chalmers publication 545746](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/545746); [RESS v260 (2025) via RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/reensy/v260y2025ics0951832025002273.html)

**Anatec (Aberdeen/Cambridge, UK): ShipRoutes and COLLRISK**
- ShipRoutes is Anatec's shipping-route database for finding traffic near proposed offshore developments (wind farms, oil and gas). Annual route movements are estimated from port-call data and from voyage information supplied by ship operators — [ScottishPower Renewables NRA Annex](https://scottishpowerrenewables.com/userfiles/file/6.3.15-1e-Volume-3-Chapter-15-Shipping-and-Navigation-Appendix-15.1-e-Annex-4.pdf)
- COLLRISK is Anatec's collision risk model. The MCA notes it has been used in other offshore wind NRAs. In one case the MCA criticised it for resting on one month of AIS data and leaving out non-AIS vessels and near-miss data — [MCA representation, EN010084](https://infrastructure.planninginspectorate.gov.uk/wp-content/ipc/uploads/projects/EN010084/EN010084-003086-Maritime%20&%20Coastguard%20Agency.pdf)
- Anatec NRAs in the UK consenting record include Rampion 2 (2023) and Outer Dowsing (2023), plus Salamander (Apr 2024), Muir Mhòr (for Fred. Olsen Seawind), Five Estuaries, Norfolk Boreas and Pentland Floating. A search summary also lists a MachairWind NRA dated April 2026 — [Rampion 2 NRA](https://infrastructure.planninginspectorate.gov.uk/wp-content/ipc/uploads/projects/EN010117/EN010117-000216-6.4.13.1%20Rampion%202%20ES%20Volume%204%20Appendix%2013.1%20Navigational%20risk%20assessment.pdf); [Outer Dowsing NRA](https://www.outerdowsing.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/6.2.15.1_Navigational-Risk-Assessment-.pdf); [Five Estuaries NRA](https://infrastructure.planninginspectorate.gov.uk/wp-content/ipc/uploads/projects/EN010115/EN010115-000408-9.10%20Navigational%20Risk%20Assessment.pdf); [Pentland Floating NRA](https://pentlandfloatingwind.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/GBPNTD-ENV-ANA-PR-00002-A.14.1-Navigational-Risk-Assessment.pdf); [marine.gov.scot NRA](https://marine.gov.scot/sites/default/files/supporting_study_14_-_navigational_risk_assessment_redacted.pdf). I did not open the documents to confirm which document belongs to which project.
- Model disagreement among UK NRA consultancies (Thanet Extension). Marico Marine's collision model was calibrated to the local baseline collision rate (1 in 6 years, from AIS) and measures the *change in encounters* without assuming consequences. Anatec's model is calibrated to national historical incident rates for at least "material damage". Run as an independent check, the two models gave different absolute return periods and different project deltas — [Vattenfall TEOW CRM review](https://infrastructure.planninginspectorate.gov.uk/wp-content/ipc/uploads/projects/EN010084/EN010084-001987-Vattenfall%20Wind%20Power%20Limited%20-%20D6_Appendix42_TEOW_CRM_RevA.pdf)
- NASH Maritime is another UK NRA author. It wrote the Morgan Offshore Wind NRA (Annex 7.1) and ran the vessel traffic surveys for Buchan Offshore Wind — [Morgan NRA](https://infrastructure.planninginspectorate.gov.uk/wp-content/ipc/uploads/projects/EN010136/EN010136-000179-F4.7.1_Morgan_Gen_ES_Navigational%20risk%20assessment.pdf); [Maritime Journal – Buchan](https://www.maritimejournal.com/?p=198516)

**MARIN SAMSON (Netherlands)**
- SAMSON (Safety Assessment Model for Shipping and Offshore in the North Sea) has been used and developed for more than 25 years. It works in two steps: first it computes *exposures* (e.g., encounters) from traffic, environmental and physics-based models, then it multiplies them by casualty rates. It covers collision, grounding, fire/explosion and contact — [MARIN SAMSON page](https://www.marin.nl/en/facilities-and-tools/software/samson); [MarineLink](https://marinelink.com/news/collision-assess-using388271)
- Traffic is built from 36 ship types × 8 size classes and comes preferably from AIS. Casualty rates are based on Lloyd's worldwide accident data for 1990–2002 — [BE-AWARE sub-report 3 (Bonn Agreement)](https://www.bonnagreement.org/site/assets/files/1129/be-aware_sub_report_3_final.pdf)
- Ownership: MARIN developed it for the Dutch Ministry of Infrastructure and the Environment, which owns it. MARIN also built a real-time risk index that applies SAMSON to live AIS — [BE-AWARE sub-report 3](https://www.bonnagreement.org/site/assets/files/1129/be-aware_sub_report_3_final.pdf)
- Applied internationally, e.g., in the Area Risk Assessment methodology for Transport Canada (oil-spill frequency) — [MARIN publication](https://www.marin.nl/publications/frequency-of-spill-model-for-area-risk-assessment-of-ship-source-oil-spills-in-canadian-waters)
- Still in active regulatory use: a MARIN collision study dated 26 May 2026 for a proposed North Sea gas platform (L07-F) uses SAMSON's ship–object contact model — [RVO: Aanvaringsstudie MARIN L7-F (2026)](https://www.rvo.nl/sites/default/files/2026-06/Aanvaringsstudie-Marin-26-mei-2026-Gaswinning-L7-F.pdf)

**DNV MARCS (Marine Accident Risk Calculation System)**
- DNV first built MARCS for UK shipping risk, and used it in the Prince William Sound study. It is a *static* model: ships keep to lanes with a Gaussian lateral distribution, traffic is an annual distribution with no seasonal variation, and collision probabilities come from fault trees based on expert judgement — [National Academies Press review](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/6126/chapter/4)
- MARCS was developed further in the EU SAFECO project (1995–98, ten partners), where it was validated against historical accident data — [PubMed abstract](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/10859782)
- DNV (as DNV GL) produced German offshore wind collision analyses under the BSH standard. One example reports a 123-year average return period between collisions, with AIS, traffic monitoring and two emergency tugs, and risk priority (RPZ) 2–3, which is acceptable — [Technische Risikoanalyse (UVP-Verbund)](https://www.uvp-verbund.de/documents-ige-ng/igc_mv/D4CA77FD-E592-4C94-BFC8-FC82F537DC5B/01.%20AO1_Technische_Risikoanalyse.pdf)

**COLLIDE (Dovre Safetec; Norwegian Continental Shelf)**
- COLLIDE came out of a research project sponsored by major oil companies and UK/Norwegian authorities. It computes collision risk for offshore installations from shipping, using risk algorithms and a traffic database. Dovre Safetec published the reference manual in 1994 ("COLLIDE – collision design criteria, phase II") — [TRID record](https://trid.trb.org/View/442722); [Hassel et al. 2020, Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1748006X20957481)
- Regulatory role: operators on the NCS must assess ship-impact risk from both field-related and passing vessels. The result sets the accidental allision load used for design. One cited criterion is fewer than 1 event per 10,000 installation-years — [Hassel et al. 2017, JoMA](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s13437-016-0123-7); [PSAM12 paper](https://www.iapsam.org/psam12/proceedings/paper/paper_266_1.pdf)
- Weaknesses: the model is widely seen as dated. It is highly sensitive to close-passing distances, and a Bayesian Belief Network replacement has been proposed. An empirical study found merchant vessels alter course around new installations, so current assessments may be too conservative — [NTNU allision model thesis](https://hdl.handle.net/11250/2722292); [PSAM12](https://www.iapsam.org/psam12/proceedings/paper/paper_266_1.pdf); [Hassel et al. 2017](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s13437-016-0123-7)

**STAPS / STAPS-cons (Chalmers University of Technology / RISE, Sweden). Not Rostock or Hamburg.**
- STAPS is a mid-fidelity simulator built on Monte Carlo runs that represents ship manoeuvrability and motion. Its authors are Axel Hörteborn and co-workers — [Chalmers 545746](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/545746)
- STAPS-cons identifies five manoeuvring failure types from AIS (loss of propulsion, loss of steering, missed turning point, and others). It runs Monte Carlo samples through a time-domain hydrodynamic simulator and maps allisions onto a set of *precalculated nonlinear FE analyses* to estimate damage and collapse probability — [Chalmers thesis 549035](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/549035); [TRID](https://trid.trb.org/View/2751949)
- Bjørnafjorden case (2026 journal article): of five scenarios, three passed and two failed the Norwegian collapse criterion (< 10⁻⁴ per year). A 20% longer missed-turn duration notably raised both allision frequency and the energy levels required — [Chalmers 549761](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/549761)

**Ship-handling and manoeuvring simulators used for bridge and channel studies**
- FORCE Technology SimFlex4 (Denmark): more than 30 years of hydrodynamic and aerodynamic modelling. The desktop engineering tool runs the DEN-Mark1 manoeuvring model, and SimFlex Cloud/Remote offers a database of about 700 ship models for engineering studies of layouts against set criteria — [FORCE cloud simulator](https://forcetechnology.com/en/services/simulations-and-cfd/cloud-based-maritime-simulator-engineering-studies); [SimFlex4 desktop brochure](https://forcetechnology.com/-/media/force-technology-media/pdf-files/4001-to-4500/4311-simflex4-desktop-engineering-studies.pdf); [FORCE ship bridge simulators](https://forcetechnology.com/en/services/ship-bridge-simulators)
- BMT REMBRANDT (UK): navigation simulation and analysis software certified by DNV to Class A/B/C/S. It is used for port and terminal design (tides, currents, UKC, squat, bank and interaction effects, tug strategies) — [BMT REMBRANDT](https://www.bmt.org/bmt-rembrandt); [BMT terminal and harbour design](https://www.bmt.org/innovations/bmt-rembrandt/simulations-to-aid-terminal-and-harbour-design)
- Bridge-specific simulator precedents: US 60 Smithland Bridge (simulation with barge captains fixed a 213.4 m main span before USCG approval); Queen Isabella Causeway (USACE ERDC real-time simulation, 2005–2007, after a 2001 tow allision); Grays Harbor (navigation span widened from 125 ft to 185 ft); Lake Lothing Third Crossing, UK (32 m clear channel, studied on a Kongsberg-based simulator at Lowestoft College) — [Smithland (Structurae)](https://structurae.de/de/fachliteratur/tagungsbeitrag/us-60-smithland-bridge-navigation-modeling); [ERDC/DTIC Queen Isabella](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA508606.pdf); [DTIC Grays Harbor](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/html/tr/ADA244084/index.html); [Lake Lothing Vessel Simulation Report](https://nsip-documents.planninginspectorate.gov.uk/published-documents/TR010023-000343-6.3%20-%20ES%20Vol%203%20-%20App%2015A%20-%20Vessel%20Simulation%20Report.pdf)

**Norwegian floating-bridge practice (E39)**
- Bjørnafjorden K12 concept: girder-impact design used explicit nonlinear FE, with impact energies taken from a risk analysis *by Rambøll* — [K12 report on kudos.dfo.no](https://kudos.dfo.no/documents/17359/files/17480.pdf)
- Column impact checks at 125 MJ and 150 MJ, described as the 10,000-year event — [Strathclyde](https://pureportal.strath.ac.uk/en/publications/ultimate-and-residual-strength-assessments-of-intact-and-collisio/)
- Nordhordland floating bridge (1994): two ship allisions so far. A reassessment combining historical-frequency analysis, IWRAP and LS-DYNA found the pontoons are the weak spot and fall short of today's requirements — [ESREL 2023](https://www.rpsonline.com.sg/proceedings/esrel2023/html/P183.html)
- Norwegian requirements say the design ship(s) should be set in a separate risk analysis covering both frequency and impact analysis — [ESREL 2021 programme (Eidem)](https://easychair.org/smart-program/ESREL2021/2021-09-21.html)

### Inferences
- **Competitive matrix (synthesis of the findings above; ratings are my judgement):**

| Tool / vendor | Modelling approach | Typical users | Licence / price | Strengths | Weaknesses a physics+AI twin can exploit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IWRAP Mk2 (IALA/GateHouse) | Analytic Fujii/MacDuff/Pedersen: geometric probability × causation factor; route legs with lateral distributions | Coastal administrations, IALA members, consultancies, bridge designers (early phase) | Basic free to IALA members; commercial about €2.8–6k per user per year | De facto standard, regulator-recognised, cheap, AIS import (commercial) | Static; overestimates allision about 4.5× vs a simulator; no consequence/FE coupling; no UQ; automation only via external XML tooling; desktop, per-user |
| Anatec COLLRISK + ShipRoutes | Encounter/route-based collision model calibrated to national incident rates; proprietary route DB | UK offshore wind developers (MGN 654 NRAs), oil and gas | Consultancy service (no software sales found) | Dominant UK NRA brand; route database | MCA criticised short AIS windows and missing non-AIS/near-miss data; results differ from Marico's on the same project (model uncertainty not quantified) |
| MARIN SAMSON | Exposure (traffic/physics) × Lloyd's casualty rates (1990–2002); 36×8 ship classes | Dutch ministry/RWS, RVO, offshore oil and gas and wind, Transport Canada | Owned by Dutch Ministry; used via MARIN studies | Long pedigree, North Sea calibration, real-time index variant | Casualty data is old; static traffic; not sold as a product |
| DNV MARCS | Static lane-based Gaussian traffic + expert-judgement fault trees | DNV advisory projects (PWS, UK, BSH wind) | Not sold; consultancy | Big-brand credibility | Annual and static, no seasonality; expert-judgement causation |
| COLLIDE (Dovre Safetec) | AIS-based passing-vessel model for offshore installations | NCS operators (PSA/Havtil compliance) | Consultancy/industry tool | Industry standard on the NCS | Dated; very sensitive to passing distance; conservative; BBN replacement proposed |
| STAPS / STAPS-cons (Chalmers/RISE) | Monte Carlo time-domain manoeuvring + AIS-derived failure modes + precalculated nonlinear FE | Academic; Bjørnafjorden case | Research code (commercial status unknown) | Closest to "physics + probabilistic" with consequences; already applied to Bjørnafjorden | Not productised; no real-time ops, no UI/automation found |
| FORCE SimFlex / BMT REMBRANDT / Kongsberg-based sims | Deterministic real-time or fast-time manoeuvring, pilots in the loop | Ports, bridge designers, pilots | Studies/licences (price not public) | High-fidelity hydrodynamics, stakeholder buy-in | Few runs means no frequency statistics; not linked to risk-frequency or structural models |

- The market is split between *frequency tools* (IWRAP, SAMSON, MARCS, COLLIDE, COLLRISK), *deterministic manoeuvring simulators*, and *structural FE* (LS-DYNA, USFOS, Abaqus). Consultants join them by hand. STAPS-cons shows academically that one pipeline can link all three. Nobody has turned it into a product yet (inferred from the absence of any commercial offering in the search results).
- IWRAP's €2.8–6k per user per year sets a low price anchor for desktop "frequency calculators". A new product should be priced as a study-accelerator or an operations service, not as a seat licence.

## Q2. Ship–bridge anti-collision and warning systems, and VTS vendors with AI features

### Takeaway
Active ship–bridge warning systems are widely deployed in China: radar + AIS + CCTV/lidar + height measurement, tiered warning zones, VHF, horns and strobes. They are driven by a 2020 national three-year hazard-remediation plan and a 2020 design code. In the US, the NTSB's finding that the Key Bridge had no warning system makes warning tech a likely AASHTO-update topic, but no US standard product exists yet. The VTS incumbents (Kongsberg Norcontrol, Saab, Wärtsilä, Frequentis, Indra, Hensoldt) are only prototyping AI (e.g., Singapore NGVTMS). None offers bridge-structure-aware risk.

### Cited Findings

**China**
- Policy: the Ministry of Transport General Office, National Railway Administration and China State Railway Group issued the *Three-Year Action Implementation Plan for Ship–Bridge Collision Hazard Remediation* (交办水〔2020〕69号). Provinces and cities (e.g., Jiangsu, Changzhou/Liyang) followed with their own action plans and working groups — [Liyang government notice](https://www.liyang.gov.cn/html/czly/2021/AQAHAIEK_0618/28139.html)
- Design code: JTG/T 3360-02-2020, *Specifications for Anti-Collision Design of Highway Bridges* (公路桥梁抗撞设计规范), issued 20 Apr 2020 and effective 1 Aug 2020, chief editor CCCC Highway Consultants. It has 7 chapters and 4 appendices covering design ship collision force, representative design ships, collision-effect calculation, structural protection facilities, and an appendix on ship–bridge collision probability and risk analysis — [MOT PDF](https://xxgk.mot.gov.cn/2020/jigou/glj/202006/P020200623699499447598.pdf)
- Deployment scale (Chinese media): all 6 bridges in the 18 km Wuhan port core zone of the Yangtze have active anti-collision warning systems; Guangxi Communications Investment Group fitted warning systems on 21 river bridges (three-level warning zones, cameras + radar, VHF, loudspeakers, strobe lights); Fuzhou's G324 Wulongjiang Bridge has high-precision radar, laser height detection and a radar–AIS fusion platform; Hangzhou Bay Bridge added an electronic-fence warning to its protection upgrade; Nanjing issued Yangtze bridge/tunnel waterway traffic safety rules. One article counts 204 ship–bridge collision accidents in China over 5 years — [The Paper](https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34075207); [163.com](https://m.163.com/dy/article/JEG483R8053802OX.html); [rmzxw.com.cn](https://www.rmzxw.com.cn/c/2023-12-18/3461819.shtml)
- Historical incidence: about 70 ship collisions with the Wuhan Yangtze River Bridge since 1957, and about 30 with the Nanjing Yangtze River Bridge — [CN107393346B patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN107393346B/en)
- Typical architecture: millimetre-wave radar (3–5 km detection), lidar, AIS, AI video, water-level sensors — [CSDN technical article](https://gitcode.csdn.net/6a2bc1f9662f9a54cb7dad72.html). Academic "proactive" systems add AI multi-sensor fusion (camera, lidar, surveillance radar, AIS, environment sensors), video restoration in rain, fog and low light, and trajectory prediction under occlusion — [Ni Liu, T&F chapter](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/oa-edit/10.1201/9781003778677-7/proactive-bridge-anti-ship-collision-warning-system-using-ai-multi-sensor-fusion-ni-liu); [Zhuang et al., vision–radar fusion pre-warning](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/oa-edit/10.1201/9781003483755-419/decision-method-ship-bridge-collision-pre-warning-based-vision-radar-data-fusion-hongyu-zhuang-dalei-wang-zhenxiong-gu-yiqing-dong-junjie-wang)
- A 2026 Chinese paper combines a floating flexible steel box fender with an active warning system and validates it by FE — [Ship & Ocean Protection journal PDF](https://csdqyfh.ijournals.cn/csdqyfh/article/pdf/251195)
- Research risk models for specific bridges: Shanghai–Nantong Yangtze Bridge (pier layout, navigation span, channel geometry; results "basically consistent" with actual risk) — [MDPI JMSE 10(11):1635](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/10/11/1635); Jintang Bridge (AIS + Monte Carlo + Bayesian network) — [MDPI Sensors 22(8):2857](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/8/2857)
- AIS-only systems are limited by latency, per a 2025 review — [MDPI Sensors 25(5):1488](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/5/1488)

**Hong Kong / HZMB**
- After the Kap Shui Mun Bridge ship collision of 23 Oct 2015, LegCo asked about HZMB anti-collision and monitoring measures. Different height restrictions apply along HKLR and TM-CLKL — [LegCo paper](https://legco.gov.hk/yr16-17/english/hc/sub_leg/sc111/papers/sc11120170614cb4-1279-1-e.pdf)
- HZMB main-bridge piers carry (German-supplied) sensors to assess impact effects quickly — [Hannover Messe](https://www.hannovermesse.de/en/news/news-articles/longest-sea-bridge-in-the-world-equipped-with-german-sensors)
- PolyU technology: AIS + vision-based monitoring that tracks traffic, evaluates routes and warns ships likely to hit the bridge, plus embedded piezoelectric sensors that measure impact force. It has been pitched as possible for HZMB, but no deployment is confirmed — [PolyU KTEO listing](https://www.polyu.edu.hk/kteo/knowledge-transfer/innovations-and-technologies/technology-search/2-construction-and-environmental-technology/2_cee_03_1113/); [Seatrade Maritime](https://www.seatrade-maritime.com/ship-operations/hk-develops-bridge-surveillance-system)

**United States: passive protection and post-Key Bridge warning technology**

## Q3. Maritime analytics and digital-twin platforms

### Takeaway
The AIS and maritime-intelligence layer has consolidated: Kpler owns MarineTraffic, FleetMon and Spire Maritime (Spire had absorbed exactEarth), and Windward was taken private by FTV Capital. ## Q4. Demand drivers: regulation, incidents, infrastructure programmes, market size

### Takeaway
The strongest near-term drivers: (1) the US. NTSB's 68-bridge Method II recommendation (Mar 2025) is now producing owner studies (Mackinac 2026, Bay Bridge, Wabasha RFQ). AASHTO and NCHRP are rewriting vessel-collision guidance, and the Key Bridge rebuild (up to $5.2B) has made pier protection a large line item. (2) UK and EU offshore wind: every project needs an NRA under MGN 654 (UK) or BSH collision analysis (DE), on a 93 GW UK pipeline. (3) NCS oil and gas: PSA/Havtil collision-risk rules (10⁻⁴). (4) China: a nationwide hazard-remediation programme plus a 2020 code. (5) Norway E39: Bjørnafjorden/Hordfast is zoned but politically uncertain, and the 10⁻⁴ collapse criterion applies. No credible market-size figure exists for "ship-collision risk software" as such. Adjacent markets (VTS, marine digital twins) are $0.6–6B with inconsistent estimates.

## Q6. Gaps current tools do not cover (UQ, optimisation, 3D, impact-physics coupling, real-time operations, AI agents)

### Takeaway
Across the landscape, no commercial product (a) couples traffic-frequency modelling, manoeuvring physics and structural consequence in one auditable pipeline; (b) quantifies model and parameter uncertainty (consultancy models disagree on the same site, and IWRAP is about 4.5× more conservative than a simulator); (c) optimises design choices such as span position, fender capacity or pier protection against risk targets; (d) carries a design-phase model into live operations as a calibrated warning twin; or (e) offers AI agents that produce regulator-ready studies. Those five gaps define the open space for a physics+AI digital-twin product.

### Cited Findings
- Static or dated cores: MARCS uses annual static traffic and expert-judgement fault trees — [NAP](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/6126/chapter/4). SAMSON's casualty rates come from 1990–2002 Lloyd's data — [BE-AWARE](https://www.bonnagreement.org/site/assets/files/1129/be-aware_sub_report_3_final.pdf). COLLIDE is considered dated, with a BBN replacement proposed — [Hassel et al. 2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1748006X20957481)
- Model-form uncertainty is large and not quantified: IWRAP allision is about 4.5× STAPS, and both are sensitive to failure duration and route layout — [Chalmers 545746](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/545746). Marico and Anatec gave different absolute collision return periods and deltas for the same wind farm — [TEOW CRM review](https://infrastructure.planninginspectorate.gov.uk/wp-content/ipc/uploads/projects/EN010084/EN010084-001987-Vattenfall%20Wind%20Power%20Limited%20-%20D6_Appendix42_TEOW_CRM_RevA.pdf). COLLIDE and the BBN model are highly sensitive to close-passing distances — [PSAM12](https://www.iapsam.org/psam12/proceedings/paper/paper_266_1.pdf)
- Data-input weaknesses: the MCA criticised a month-long AIS window and the absence of non-AIS and near-miss data — [MCA EN010084](https://infrastructure.planninginspectorate.gov.uk/wp-content/ipc/uploads/projects/EN010084/EN010084-003086-Maritime%20&%20Coastguard%20Agency.pdf). AIS latency limits warning systems — [MDPI Sensors 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/5/1488)
- Consequence coupling exists only in research or by hand: STAPS-cons uses precalculated nonlinear FE with Monte Carlo — [Chalmers 549035](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/549035). Nordhordland used IWRAP plus LS-DYNA separately — [ESREL 2023](https://www.rpsonline.com.sg/proceedings/esrel2023/html/P183.html). BSH lets the FE method be chosen freely, with proof of applicability — [BSH](https://digitale-bibliothek.bsh.bund.de/viewer/fulltext/179029/91/)
- Sensitivity of results to behavioural assumptions: a 20% longer missed-turn duration moved Bjørnafjorden scenarios notably — [Chalmers 549761](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/549761). Vessels avoid new installations, so static assessments may be too conservative — [Hassel et al. 2017](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s13437-016-0123-7)
- Automation: IWRAP model building from AIS depends on external DaMSA tooling (XML) — [IALA wiki](https://www.iala.int/wiki/iwrap/index.php/Processing_AIS_Data)
- Operations: deployed warning systems (China) use tiered geofence zones with VHF, horn and strobe alerts — [The Paper](https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34075207). VTS AI is still at prototype stage (MPA NGVTMS) — [Kongsberg 2024](https://www.kongsberg.com/newsroom/stories/2024/5/ai-enabled-next-generation-vessel-traffic-management-system-in-singapore). The US had no warning system at the Key Bridge — [WSET](https://wset.com/news/nation-world/ntsb-vote-probable-cause-dali-crash-key-bridge-baltimore)
- AI agents in maritime focus on compliance and screening (Windward MAI Expert), not engineering risk studies — [Windward](https://windward.ai/solutions/mai-expert/)

### Inferences
- **Gap → product feature map (synthesis):**
  1. *UQ:* deliver frequency and collapse-probability **distributions**, not point estimates (parameter + model-form + AIS-sampling uncertainty), and reconcile IWRAP-style and simulation-based estimates. This directly answers the 4.5× and Marico-vs-Anatec spread.
  2. *Physics coupling:* AIS-derived failure modes → fast manoeuvring (surrogate of a SimFlex/STAPS-class model) → impact energy/force → surrogate FE (pontoon/girder/pier capacity) → collapse probability vs 10⁻⁴ (N400/Eurocode) or the AASHTO Method II threshold. Floating bridges need dynamic global response, which the analytic tools cannot do.
  3. *Optimisation:* search over span location, navigation channel geometry, fender/dolphin sizing, speed limits, escort-tug rules and closure triggers to meet a risk target at minimum cost. None of the incumbents offer this.
  4. *3D twin:* 3D Tiles / iTwin-compatible geometry (bridge, bathymetry, air-gap), so owners on Bentley or Cesium can adopt it.
  5. *Real-time ops:* reuse the calibrated design model to drive consequence-aware warnings (probability × energy vs capacity), traffic-stop triggers for road users (the NTSB lesson) and VTS plug-ins, with measured false-alarm and miss rates.
  6. *AI agents:* generate MGN 654 NRA, AASHTO Method II and N400 documentation, run scenario batches, and answer regulator queries with traceable assumptions. This is unclaimed (Windward-type agents serve compliance only).
- Main competitive threats: (a) Chalmers/RISE commercialising STAPS-cons; (b) MARIN extending SAMSON with its simulators; (c) DNV or Kongsberg bundling with VTS or classification services; (d) Chinese integrators adding risk analytics to their installed warning systems. All of this is speculative; none was observed in the sources.

### Gaps
- No systematic review retrieved that inventories UQ or optimisation in ship–bridge risk tools (the search budget ran out). The gap claims above rest on the absence of such features in the tool descriptions found, not on a comprehensive feature audit.
- Vendor roadmaps (GateHouse, MARIN, DNV, Kongsberg, Saab) for AI or physics coupling are unknown.
