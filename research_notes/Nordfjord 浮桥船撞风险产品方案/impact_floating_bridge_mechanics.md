## 1. Impact energy: kinetic energy with added mass, external mechanics (Minorsky, Pedersen & Zhang 1998, Liu & Amdahl), energy partition, angle/eccentricity

### Takeaway
Available energy is E₀ = ½·M·(1+mₐ)·V². Use about 5–10 % added mass for surge (bow/stern) and about 40 % for sway (broadside); NORSOK N-003:2017 gives 10 %/40 %. A compliant floating bridge takes only part of this energy into local crushing; the rest becomes global bridge and ship motion. Pedersen & Zhang (1998) give closed-form expressions for the energy released and the impulse in 2D. Liu & Amdahl extended this to 3D. Decoupled external mechanics works well for near-perpendicular, short collisions. It loses accuracy for glancing or skew impacts at small angles and for long-duration contact, which is exactly the regime of a soft, long-period floating bridge.

### Cited Findings
- **Added mass.** NORSOK N-003 (2017) says that without operational restrictions a hydrodynamic added mass of **40 % for sideways** and **10 % for bow and stern** impact can be assumed. Together with the larger standard vessel, this gives a design collision energy of about **50 MJ**, up from **11 MJ (head-on) and 14 MJ (broadside)** under the old practice. — [Assessment of ship impact risk – new NORSOK N-003 guidelines (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317340722_Assessment_of_ship_impact_risk_to_offshore_structures_-_New_NORSOK_N-003_guidelines); [UiS thesis: Assessment of ship collision according to new NORSOK N-003](https://uis.brage.unit.no/uis-xmlui/handle/11250/3148899). The source search summary did not give the exact clause.
- A **7,500 t supply vessel** is now the standard offshore design striker. It is used for FPSO side and stern collisions with 40 % added mass — [Structural damage of ship–FPSO collisions (J. Marine Eng. & Tech., 2017)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20464177.2017.1359387). NTNU has compared LS-DYNA bow and stern indentation for two 7,500 t supply vessels against the DNV-GL RP-C204 design curves — [Analysis and design of offshore tubular members against ship impacts (NTNU Open)](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/2468983).
- **Pedersen & Zhang (1998)**, "On impact mechanics in ship collisions", *Marine Structures* 11(10):429–449. It gives **analytical closed-form expressions for the energy released for crushing and for the impact impulse**. It covers ship–ship collisions, collisions with rigid walls, and collisions with **flexible offshore structures**, includes **friction at the contact point**, and agrees well with time-domain simulations. — [DTU Orbit record](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/on-impact-mechanics-in-ship-collisions-2/). Later book treatment: Zhang, Pedersen & Villavicencio (2019), *Probability and Mechanics of Ship Collision and Grounding*, Elsevier — [Elsevier](https://shop.elsevier.com/books/probability-and-mechanics-of-ship-collision-and-grounding/zhang/978-0-12-815022-1).
- **Minorsky (1959).** From 26 collisions he split the problem into external dynamics and internal mechanics. He fitted E ≈ **47.2·R_T + 32.7 (E in MJ, R_T = damaged steel volume in m³)**. The method assumes a fully inelastic, 1D collision governed by momentum conservation. It is only a rough global estimate and can be misread as "resistance scales with steel volume". — [NTU (Taiwan) paper](https://scholars.lib.ntu.edu.tw/bitstreams/9391e4ed-b925-4615-b4b7-3f84fad580a1/download); [EAI Endorsed Trans. paper (imperial-unit form, coefficients 414.5 / 121.9)](https://eudl.eu/pdf/10.4108/eetsmre.9723); [Dynalook 2001](https://www.dynalook.com/conferences/european-conf-2001/estimation-of-the-transverse-crush-resistance-of-a.pdf).
- **Liu & Amdahl (2010)**, "A new formulation of the impact mechanics of ship collisions and its application to a ship–iceberg collision", *Marine Structures*. It extends Stronge's impact theory to **3D in a local contact coordinate system**, so contact-surface orientation and off-centre eccentricity can be included. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0951833910000237); 3D-extension description in [EAI paper](https://eudl.eu/pdf/10.4108/eetsmre.9723). Related validation work: "Impact mechanics of ship collisions and validations with experimental results" (*Marine Structures*) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0951833916300673).
- **Yu, Liu & Amdahl (2019, *Ships and Offshore Structures*)** checked the assumptions of decoupled external dynamics against coupled planar 3-DOF and 6-DOF simulations. The decoupled approach is **reasonable for right-angle collisions** but **loses accuracy for skew collisions at small angles and for long-duration collisions**. — [NTNU Open 11250/2590974](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/2590974).
- The NTNU PhD thesis *Hydrodynamic and structural aspects of ship collisions* (2017) reports that the decoupled method is simple and fast. It misses **secondary impacts** that appear in coupled simulations once heave, roll and pitch are included. — [NTNU Open 11250/2461937](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/2461937). (The search summary attributed this to "Amdahl"; I believe it is Zhaolong Yu's PhD under Amdahl. Verify.)
- A 3D simplified analytical method (MATLAB) dissipates **less energy than the 2D method**. **Collision height** matters a lot, and setting the restitution-related coefficient to zero is **unsafe for collision angles > 90°**. — [Liu & Hu, Chinese J. Ship Research 2017](https://english.ship-research.com/en/article/doi/10.3969/j.issn.1673-3185.2017.02.011).
- On a floating bridge (Bjørnafjorden K12 concept), **local plastic dissipation is about 40 % of the available kinetic energy for a head-on pontoon impact, about 60 % at 45° offset and about 80 % at 80° offset**. Pontoon impacts "yield increasing local deformation for increasing deviation". — [Statens vegvesen, Preferred solution K12 (Brage)](https://vegvesen.brage.unit.no/vegvesen-xmlui/handle/11250/2660035). These figures come from a search summary of that record.
- Assuming all collision energy is absorbed locally **overestimates local deformation**, because global bridge motion also takes up energy. — summarised from follow-up work citing [Sha, Amdahl & Dørum, JOMAE 141(3):031601](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17445302.2023.2195242).

### Inferences
- **Core external-mechanics equations for the ROM** (derived from standard rigid-body impact mechanics, consistent with the DNV-RP-C204 / Pedersen–Zhang framework; verify against the primary texts):
  - Fixed rigid target, central impact normal to the surface: E_loc = ½·M(1+mₐ)·V².
  - Compliant or free-floating target, 1D, perfectly plastic, target initially at rest (the "reduced-mass" form; DNV-RP-C204 gives the same structure for compliant installations):
    `E_loc = ½·m_s'·V_s²·(1 − V_t/V_s)² / (1 + m_s'/m_t')`, with V_t = 0. This gives `E_loc = ½·m_s'·V_s²·m_t'/(m_s'+m_t')`,
    where m_s' = M_s(1+mₐ,s) and m_t' is the **effective (generalised) mass of the struck bridge at the contact point**, including pontoon added mass.
  - Eccentric 2D/3D impact (Pedersen–Zhang / Liu–Amdahl structure), sticking contact: E_loc = ½·v_n²/D, where D = Σ_bodies [ n·(M⁻¹)·n + (r×n)²/(I(1+j)) ] sums the translational and rotational compliances of both bodies along the contact normal n. Sliding (friction μ below the critical value) lowers E_loc further. Eccentricity raises the rotational terms, which lowers the energy absorbed locally.
- **Applied to the K12 split.** In the impulsive limit, 40 % local dissipation in head-on pontoon impact implies m_t'/m_s' ≈ 0.67. 80 % at 80° offset implies m_t'/m_s' ≈ 4. This fits the idea that a curved, end-anchored bridge is far stiffer and more "massive" along its axis than transversely, so oblique hits put more energy into local crushing. This is my interpretation and has not been confirmed in the source text.
- A floating bridge has long horizontal periods (tens of seconds; not verified here), while ship–pontoon contact lasts seconds. That puts the bridge in the regime the decoupled method handles worst, according to Yu, Liu & Amdahl (long duration). A **time-domain coupled ROM (ship rigid body + nonlinear contact spring + modal bridge)** is therefore preferable to a closed-form energy split, at least for pontoon impacts.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the exact Pedersen–Zhang (1998) closed-form expressions, including the friction and sliding cases and the compliant-structure version. The 2019 Zhang–Pedersen–Villavicencio book should be the next source.
- I found no sourced added-mass coefficients specific to floating-bridge pontoons. Frequency-dependent added mass for pontoons comes from WAMIT/WADAM-type panel codes. AASHTO's C_H (often quoted as about 1.05 for large under-keel clearance to 1.25 for small) is from memory and was not verified this session.

## 2. Force–deformation: DNV-RP-C204, Pedersen et al. (1993) bow formula, AASHTO / EN 1991-1-7 equivalent static forces, large ships, deckhouse impacts

### Takeaway
DNV-RP-C204 (Sept 2019, amended Sept 2021) is the latest edition found. It defines force–deformation curves for the 7,500 t standard supply vessel (bow, stern, broadside) and the ductile / shared-energy / strength-design logic, but it is offshore-oriented. For large merchant and cruise bows hitting a rigid structure, the Pedersen et al. (1993) empirical formula (also adopted in EN 1991-1-7 Annex C) is the usual reference. It gives forces of hundreds of MN for 400–1,700 MJ impacts, roughly twice AASHTO's equivalent static force. Superstructure (deckhouse/forecastle) impacts on girders are a separate failure mode. Deckhouses are soft, absorb most of the energy, and their force depends strongly on the vertical impact position. AASHTO handles them with a reduction factor on the bow force.

### Cited Findings
- **DNV-RP-C204, "Structural design against accidental loads": current edition 2019-09, amended 2021-09.** No 2023 or 2024 edition was found. — [DNV page](https://www.dnv.com/energy/standards-guidelines/dnv-rp-c204-structural-design-against-accidental-loads/).
- The 2019 revision "made considerable changes on the design against ship collisions". It updated the **design collision energy and force–displacement curves of the standard supply vessel**, ship–installation interaction, the **compactness criterion of offshore tubulars**, and the **external dynamic models**. — [Amdahl & Yu, "Design against ship collisions in accordance with the new DNV RP C204" (NTNU Open)](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/2987973); [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355486999_Design_against_ship_collisions_in_accordance_with_the_new_DNV_RP_C204). An NTNU "transition indentation ratio" provides theoretical support for the R_c compactness criterion — [NTNU Open](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/2468983).
- **Pedersen et al. (1993)**, "Ship impacts: bow collisions", Publ. No. 821, 3rd Int. Symp. on Structural Crashworthiness and Failure, Liverpool. This is an empirical bow-crushing formula fitted to numerical results for **six merchant ships**, valid for about **500–300,000 DWT**. Its inputs are kinetic energy (displacement and initial speed) and vessel length. — [Marine Structures 2025, "Ship bow impact force in head-on collision"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0951833925001637); [DTU Orbit](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/ship-bow-impact-force-in-head-on-collision/); [Study on axial crushing force of bulbous bow (Ocean Eng. 2022)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0029801822007946).
  - Constants in secondary sources: **P₀ = 210 MN, L̄ = L_pp/275 m, Ē = E_imp/1425 MNm**. It is piecewise, with the threshold Ē ≥ L̄^2.6 (the printed text was garbled). — [Marine Structures 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0951833925001637).
  - Pedersen's formula gives **impact forces about twice those from AASHTO** — [Scribd copy of a design document (secondary)](https://www.scribd.com/doc/62424860/sdr561).
- **Pedersen (2020), "Design of bridges against ship collisions"** (*Marine Structures*; DTU) derives load–displacement relations for **six vessel types and tonnages crushing against rigid walls**. It fits an empirical design relation and argues that existing standards (AASHTO/Eurocode) need updating. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0951833920301040); [DTU PDF](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/218275168/Design_of_bridges_against_Ship_Collisions.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343462915_Design_of_bridges_against_ship_collisions). - **EN 1991-1-7:2006**, Annex C (informative): dynamic design for impact, ship classification, frontal and lateral forces. The national annex sets the NDPs. — [EN 1991-1-7:2006 copy](https://www.phd.eng.br/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/en.1991.1.7.2006.pdf). ### Inferences
- **Pedersen et al. (1993) / EN 1991-1-7 Annex C bow formula (reconstructed)**:
  - `P_bow = P₀·L̄·[Ē + (5.0 − L̄)·L̄^1.6]^0.5` for Ē ≥ L̄^2.6
  - `P_bow = 2.24·P₀·[Ē·L̄]^0.5` for Ē < L̄^2.6
  - with P₀ = 210 MN, L̄ = L_pp/275 m, Ē = E_imp/1425 MNm, and E_imp = ½·m_x·V₀², where m_x is the mass including about 5 % surge added mass.
  - Maximum indentation: `s_max = π·E_imp/(2·P_bow)`.
  - Check: the two branches meet exactly at Ē = L̄^2.6, since both reduce to √5·L̄^1.8 ≈ 2.24·L̄^1.8. That supports this reading of the garbled snippet, but it should still be checked against the EN 1991-1-7 text.
- **Worked values (my calculation).** A cruise ship of about 87,300 t with L_pp ≈ 300 m (L_pp assumed) hitting a rigid wall: 6 kn → E ≈ 437 MJ, P ≈ 270 MN, s ≈ 2.5 m. 10 kn → about 1,213 MJ, 450 MN, 4.2 m. 12 kn → about 1,747 MJ, 545 MN, 5.0 m. A compliant floating bridge sees lower peak force and lower local energy than this.
- Collision duration for roughly constant crushing force against a rigid target: t ≈ 2·s_max/V₀, i.e. about **1.5–2 s** for these cases. On a compliant bridge, contact lasts longer.
- **Recommended curve library for the ROM**:
  - (i) DNV-RP-C204 supply-vessel curves (bow, stern, side) for service vessels;
  - (ii) a Pedersen-type rigid-wall bow curve scaled by L_pp and energy, for merchant and cruise bows (bulbous-bow first peak, then roughly constant mean crushing force);
  - (iii) deckhouse/forecastle curves from Sha et al. (soft, position-dependent);
  - (iv) pontoon-wall resistance curves (RC or prestressed; see §3).
  - Combine (i)–(iii) with (iv) using the shared-energy logic (the softer body crushes first). The DNV ductile / shared-energy / strength-design regime classification is from memory and was not verified this session.

## 3. Floating-bridge collision research (NTNU, SINTEF/MARINTEK, Statens vegvesen E39): pontoon and girder impacts, global response, coupled analysis, key findings

### Takeaway
The NTNU/UiS group (Sha, Amdahl, Dørum, Yu) set up the standard workflow: LS-DYNA local models produce force–deformation curves, which feed USFOS nonlinear global dynamic bridge models. Later work adds wind and waves. Key findings:
- The girder of a curved, end-anchored bridge can withstand about 250 MJ girder impacts.
- Deckhouse–girder impacts damage mainly the deckhouse.
- Pontoon damage is acceptable for flooding, but columns and column connections govern.
- Damping hardly matters because peak response comes soon after impact.
- Global motion takes a large share of the energy in head-on pontoon impacts.

### Cited Findings
- **Sha & Amdahl et al., "Dynamic responses of a floating bridge subjected to ship collision load on bridge girders"** (Procedia Engineering, EURODYN 2017). A **20,000 t ship at 5 m/s gives about 250 MJ**. The girders **can withstand the 250 MJ** from a normal-strength cruise ship, and the **curved design raises transverse girder stiffness, which helps resist collision**. Local damage was modelled in LS-DYNA and global response in USFOS. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1877705817339176); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319654389_Dynamic_responses_of_a_floating_bridge_subjected_to_ship_collision_load_on_bridge_girders).
- **Sha, Amdahl & Dørum, "Local and global responses of a floating bridge under ship–girder collisions"**, *J. Offshore Mech. Arct. Eng.* 141(3):031601 (2018/2019; sources disagree on the year). A cruise-ship **forecastle hits a steel floating-bridge girder**. LS-DYNA local results give F–δ curves, which are used in USFOS global analysis. — summarised via [Sha et al. 2023 (SOS)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17445302.2023.2195242) and [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-97-5714-5_1).

Ship impact **governs most columns**, and **column and connection detailing is heavily governed by ship impact** — [K12 fatigue assessment](https://kudos.dfo.no/documents/17854/files/17952.pdf).
- **Eldegard (NTNU MSc).** A **1,250 MJ** impact on the pontoon nearest the navigation channel. A range of energies was used because of design-energy uncertainty. **Damping changed results by only a few percent, since the critical response occurs shortly after impact.** — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Analysis-and-Design-of-Floating-Bridge-over-Bridge-Eldegard/a9f8f1f4f101c6c1c65b0255618f5413112a07f6).
- **Recent reviews.**
  - "A review on design and analysis of floating bridges: numerical simulations, model tests and field measurements" (Ocean Eng. 2024) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0029801824014033).
  - "Analysis and Design of Floating Bridges: Recent Advances and Challenges" (Springer 2025). It cites a **Rambøll (2020) detailed ship-impact results report** and an **Aker Solutions repair-after-ship-impact study (2021/2024)** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-96-5435-2_5).
  - Parametric study of curved discrete-pontoon bridges — [T&F 2021](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17445302.2021.1943849).
- **Fluid effects in ship–bridge collision.** Fluid-field influence on impact force — [JMSE 13(8):1573 (2025)](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse13081573); FSI-based ship–bridge collision — [Ocean Eng. 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0029801825015562); review of assessment methods — [2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0263823124007882).

### Inferences
- **When global response governs.**
  - (a) Head-on, perpendicular pontoon impacts on a transversely flexible bridge: global motion takes about 60 % of the energy, so girder bending (weak axis), axial force and mooring are the checks.
  - (b) Oblique impacts load the stiff axial / arch direction: local crushing takes up to about 80 %, so pontoon and column local damage and flooding govern.
  - (c) Deckhouse/forecastle–girder impacts: the soft deckhouse takes > 90 %, so peak force is capped by deckhouse crushing strength, and girder local/global checks follow from that force cap.
- Peak response comes shortly after impact and damping matters little (Eldegard; K12). So a ROM can use **undamped or lightly damped modal dynamics over a short window (one to two fundamental periods)**. That greatly reduces cost and supports surrogate training.
- The validated workflow (LS-DYNA F–δ, then a global beam/shell model in USFOS, SIMO/RIFLEX or OrcaFlex) is the natural template for the product ROM. Swap the USFOS global model for a modal reduced model calibrated to the bridge's eigenmodes.

### Gaps
- No numbers were retrieved for Sha et al. (2019 JOMAE) or Sha et al. (2023 SOS): peak forces, girder moments, mooring forces, contact duration.
- No specific SINTEF/MARINTEK (SIMO/RIFLEX coupled collision) publications, or Lu/Moan papers on floating-bridge collisions, were found.
- No published "effective bridge mass at pontoon" values were found. These must come from the project's modal model.

## 4. Bjørnafjorden (K12/K7) collision design; Bergsøysund and Nordhordland

### Takeaway
Bjørnafjorden concept studies (2016–2020) used girder-impact energies of about 250 MJ (20,000 t at 5 m/s) and pontoon cases from an 8,000 t supply vessel up to 1,250 MJ. They found up to 200 MJ dissipated locally in the worst case. Ship impact drives column and connection design; pontoon flooding is acceptable; deckhouse–girder damage is limited. The final design-basis energies could not be confirmed. Nordhordland (1994) was reassessed: its concrete pontoons cannot withstand a head-on impact of about 25 MJ without strengthening. That is an order of magnitude below modern cruise-ship energies.

### Cited Findings
- **Bjørnafjorden concept.** End-anchored, curved floating bridge on pontoons with supplementary side mooring (K12). Ship-collision energies studied include **250 MJ** (girder) and **1,250 MJ** (pontoon near channel). — [Semantic Scholar (Eldegard)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Analysis-and-Design-of-Floating-Bridge-over-Bridge-Eldegard/a9f8f1f4f101c6c1c65b0255618f5413112a07f6); [Procedia 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1877705817339176).
- **K12 results:** up to **200 MJ** dissipated locally (worst case); pontoon flooding acceptable; columns more critical; deckhouse–girder damage limited; local dissipation 40/60/80 % with offset angle — [Statens vegvesen Brage K12](https://vegvesen.brage.unit.no/vegvesen-xmlui/handle/11250/2660035).
- **K12 design consequence:** column and connection detailing is heavily governed by ship impact; moment–rotation (plastic hinge) curves are needed for columns — [K12 summary](https://kudos.dfo.no/documents/565/files/540.pdf); [fatigue report](https://kudos.dfo.no/documents/17854/files/17952.pdf).
- **Multi-hazard case:** an 8,000 t supply vessel hits pontoon A3 next to the channel (high bridge) — [SOS 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17445302.2023.2195242).
- **Nordhordland Bridge.** The UiS study found the **current pontoon design does not have enough capacity for a head-on impact of about 25 MJ**. Prestressed concrete pontoons were modelled in **LS-DYNA**, five scenarios, including **carbon-fibre (CFRP) strengthening**. — [UiS Brage 11250/3087151](https://uis.brage.unit.no/uis-xmlui/handle/11250/3087151). A related paper, "Ship Allision Risk Analysis for the 28-Year-Old Nordhordland Bridge in Norway" (Sha, Moen, Flage), turned up in the same search. Bridge background — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordhordland_Bridge).
- **Bergsøysund:** the steel superstructure rests on **7 lightweight concrete pontoons**; it is end-supported (no side mooring) and instrumented for monitoring. — [Structural monitoring of an end-supported pontoon bridge (Marine Structures 2016)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0951833916300284); [NTNU floating bridges research](https://www.ntnu.edu/kt/research/dynamics/research/long-span/floating-bridges). No Bergsøysund collision-design energy was found.

### Inferences
- Design energies have risen from tens of MJ (1990s Nordhordland/Bergsøysund) to hundreds of MJ (Bjørnafjorden), while cruise-ship energies at 6–12 kn are about 0.4–1.8 GJ (§7). A Nordfjord-type crossing with cruise traffic therefore needs either traffic control (speed limits, tugs, routing) or acceptance that a full-speed cruise-ship strike exceeds local capacity. The risk product should treat **speed at impact** as its dominant variable, since energy scales with V².
- The 250 MJ girder benchmark matches a 20,000 t ship at 5 m/s (no added mass). For girder impacts by much bigger cruise ships, the force cap from deckhouse crushing strength (not the energy) is likely the governing parameter.

### Gaps
- **The Bjørnafjorden design basis** (SBJ-01-C4-SVV-01-BA-001 or later), with design vessel types, MJ per scenario and annual-frequency thresholds, was **not retrieved**. Nor were reports SBJ-33-C5-OON-22-RE-013/-014/-015 or the Rambøll 2020 detailed impact report. The navigation-channel clearance and width were not confirmed. All of these are high priority for verification.
- K7 vs K12 collision-specific differences were not found.
- Original collision-design criteria for Bergsøysund (1992) and Nordhordland (1994) were not found, apart from the about 25 MJ reassessment.

## 5. Reduced-order and surrogate approaches; digital twins; when high-fidelity FE is needed

### Takeaway
ML surrogates trained on FE collision runs are established for ship–ship damage and for barge/ship–pier impact force (ANN, RBF, RSM, LSTM/Transformer; 2016–2026). Reported accuracy is moderate (R² about 0.7–0.8 for ship-collision damage dimensions) with millisecond-to-0.1 s inference. I found no floating-bridge-specific ML surrogate or PINN for impact. The physically sound ROM is: rigid-body ship + nonlinear contact spring (F–δ library) + modal (or reduced FE) floating bridge with pontoon added mass and mooring stiffness, integrated over a short time window. FE is reserved for calibrating F–δ curves, pontoon breach/flooding, column/connection plasticity and superstructure–girder impacts.

### Cited Findings
- **Hybrid deep-learning surrogate (2025, *Eng. Appl. of AI*).** Uses AIS to identify collision scenarios; damage comes from **Super-Element** simulations. It predicts damage length, height and penetration with LSTM-Transformer/Transformer/MLP/RNN variants, **R² ≈ 0.72–0.79, about 0.1 s per prediction** (Gulf of Finland, ship–ship). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0952197625001587); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/388842105_A_hybrid_deep_learning_method_for_the_real-time_prediction_of_collision_damage_consequences_in_operational_conditions). Cites **Silionis & Anyfantis (2022)**, an ANN trained on FEM data for ship damage.
- **ANN + Monte Carlo (2016, SOS).** An ANN trained on time-domain collision simulations **replaces repeated FEM runs inside Monte Carlo simulation** — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17445302.2016.1258759).
- **ML + FFT (2023, Ocean Eng.)** predicts **full impact-force time histories** (duration and frequency content) for pier collisions — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0029801823017316).
- **Response-surface force–damage-depth model (2024)** for bulbous bow vs. pylon: larger forces with higher pile cap and smaller pylon curvature — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1350630724010094).
- **Real-time ML ship/barge–pier collision prediction** (Haq et al., *Struct. Control Health Monit.*, 2026). Trained on FEM; predicts force, displacement, stress and energy absorption **in milliseconds** — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/stc/5568505).
- **RBF neural network** (older) for ship–bridge collision force, with inputs ship mass and velocity — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/225507367_Calculation_method_of_ship_collision_force_on_bridge_using_artificial_neural_network). Low-sample surrogates (4 types) for berthing force — [JMSE 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/12/6/898). Ship–bridge collision **risk** via surrogate safety measures plus bivariate EVT — [RESS 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0951832025011780).
- **Methods from adjacent fields** (automotive crash): CarCrashNet dataset and hierarchical neural solver — [arXiv 2605.07098](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07098); Mask-Morph Graph U-Net mesh surrogate — [arXiv 2605.15231](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15231).
- **Bergsøysund** has a long-term structural-monitoring system (an end-supported pontoon bridge) — [Marine Structures 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0951833916300284); [NTNU](https://www.ntnu.edu/kt/research/dynamics/research/long-span/floating-bridges).
- Validated precedent for a two-level local/global split: LS-DYNA F–δ curves feeding USFOS — [Procedia 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1877705817339176). Decoupled external mechanics is inaccurate for skew, long-duration impacts — [Yu, Liu & Amdahl 2019](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/2590974).

### Inferences
- **Proposed ROM formulation** (derived, not from a single source):
  - Ship: `m_s'·ẍ_s = −F_c(δ)`, with δ = x_s − u_b(x_c)·n and m_s' = M(1+mₐ).
  - Bridge, N modes q_i: `M_i·q̈_i + C_i·q̇_i + K_i·q_i = φ_i(x_c)·n·F_c(δ)`. Here K_i includes hydrostatic stiffness and mooring stiffness (linearised or a nonlinear catenary lookup), and M_i includes pontoon added mass at the relevant period.
  - Contact: `F_c(δ)` from the F–δ library, with an unloading branch (elastic rebound slope) and a sticking/sliding friction switch for oblique impacts.
  - Outputs: E_loc = ∫F_c dδ, peak F_c, contact time, modal peaks, and from them girder M_y, M_z, N and mooring tension.
  - Effective mass at the contact point: `1/m_eff = Σ φ_i(x_c)²/M_i`. Use it for a quick closed-form check: `E_loc ≈ ½·m_s'·V²·m_eff/(m_s'+m_eff)` when contact time ≪ T_1.
- **Speed.** About 10–30 bridge modes plus a 1-DOF ship, integrated over about 60–120 s at Δt ≈ 0.01 s, runs in well under a second. That allows Monte Carlo over the AIS-derived (V, θ, x_c, ship type) distribution. An ML surrogate is then optional. It is useful mainly for FE-level quantities (penetration, breach area, column plastic rotation) learned offline from LS-DYNA/Abaqus runs on a design-of-experiments grid. This mirrors the 2016 ANN+MCS and 2025 hybrid-DL approaches.
- **When high-fidelity FE (LS-DYNA/Abaqus) is needed** (from §2–§4 findings):
  - (1) to generate or calibrate F–δ curves for new striker types: cruise bows and superstructures, which have no published curves;
  - (2) pontoon wall penetration and breach, i.e. watertightness and flooding: RC or prestressed walls, the shared-energy regime;
  - (3) columns and pontoon–column connections where section forces exceed elastic capacity (K12 needed moment–rotation curves);
  - (4) deckhouse/forecastle vs. girder, where the vertical impact position gives large force variation and the rigid-girder assumption must be checked;
  - (5) oblique or glancing impacts with long contact and secondary impacts (6-DOF coupling);
  - (6) any case where the ROM puts a member in the strength-design or shared-energy regime near capacity;
  - (7) post-impact residual-strength and progressive-failure checks (flooded pontoon plus environmental loads).
- **PINN option.** Embed the modal equations as a physics loss and learn only the contact law and damage outputs. This was not found in the literature for floating bridges, so it is a novel R&D element.

### Gaps
- No published **PINN for ship impact** or **ML surrogate for floating-bridge collision** was found. No published **digital twin of Bergsøysund with collision functionality** was found; only monitoring papers. The NTNU SFI / digital-twin programme details were not retrieved.
- Accuracy and benchmark data for the cited ML surrogates on bridge impact force (error metrics) were not retrieved, except the R² for the 2025 ship–ship study.

## 6. Real incidents as validation (Dali / Key Bridge 2024 and others; floating-bridge strikes)

### Takeaway
Dali (about 112,000 t displacement, 6.4 kn) struck the NW column of Pier 17. Its kinetic energy was about 0.6 GJ. NTSB's AASHTO-based equivalent static force was more than 4× (press reports: "nearly 5×") the pier's lateral capacity of 5,509 kips (about 24.5 MN), i.e. above about 98 MN. Pedersen's formula would give about 320 MN. This is a useful order-of-magnitude validation case for a bow-force model on a rigid pier, but not for floating-bridge global response. The only floating-bridge ship strike found is a container ship hitting a floating bridge on the Suez Canal. No strikes at Hood Canal, Lake Washington or Norwegian floating bridges were found.

### Cited Findings
- **Dali kinematics and mass:** displacement **112,383 t** as loaded at departure; draft **12.15 m**; LOA **984 ft** (one report says 947 ft). The starboard bow struck the **NW column of Pier 17 at 6.4 kn** (final report) / **6.5 kn** (preliminary). Speed over ground was **8.6 kn** earlier in the sequence. — [NTSB MIR-25-40](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/MIR2540.pdf); [NTSB preliminary report](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Documents/DCA24MM031_PreliminaryReport%203.pdf); [MDTA party submission](https://data.ntsb.gov/Docket/Document/docBLOB?ID=19326223&FileExtension=pdf&FileName=NTSB+-+MDTA+Party+Submission-FSK+Allision_9.19.2025_Redacted-Rel.pdf).
- **Force vs. capacity:** NTSB used the **equivalent static force method of the 2009 AASHTO Guide Specification**. The estimated impact force was **more than four times Pier 17's lateral capacity of 5,509 kips** (capacity from federal calculations). The exact force value was cut off in the snippet. — [NTSB MIR-25-40](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/MIR2540.pdf). CNN reported investigators said "**nearly five times**" the capacity — [CNN 2025-11-18](https://www.cnn.com/2025/11/18/us/ntsb-dali-bridge-collapse).
- **Protection and consequences:** dolphins and fendering did **not withstand** the impact. Six spans (17–22) collapsed. Contributing cause: no countermeasures reducing vulnerability. NTSB identified **68 bridges in 19 states**, built before 1991, without a current vulnerability assessment. — [NTSB press release](https://www.ntsb.gov/news/press-releases/Pages/NR20251118.aspx); [NTSB MIR-25-10, "Safeguarding Bridges from Vessel Strikes" (Mar 2025)](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/MIR2510.pdf); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/11/18/us/ntsb-dali-bridge-collapse); [CRS FAQ R48028](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48028).
- **Press engineering estimates** (low reliability):
  - New York Times analysis, via a blog: "upward of 100 million newtons"; ignores rotation and angle — [Autosopedia](https://autosopedia.com/blog/2024/03/30/baltimore-bridge-impact-had-the-brute-force-of-a-rocket-launch/).
  - Impulse estimate assuming a 1 s contact: about 26.4 million lbf, about 117 MN — [Modern Sciences](https://modernsciences.org/dali-hit-key-bridge-with-the-force-of-66-heavy-trucks-at-highway-speed/).
- **Floating-bridge strike:** the container ship **One Orpheus**, with a rudder malfunction, struck the **Martyr Ahmed El-Mansy floating bridge** (Ismailia, Suez Canal). Four tugs recovered the ship and it resumed its voyage after rudder repair. — [Bridge Design & Engineering](https://www.bridgeweb.com/Container-ship-strikes-floating-bridge/9264). The date and bridge damage were not given in the snippet.
- **Hood Canal Bridge:** opened in 1961 and **sank in 1979** (not attributed to a ship strike in these results) — [Hacker News comment (low reliability)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39825560).

### Inferences
- **Dali energy (my calculation):** ½·112,383 t·(3.29 m/s)² ≈ **610 MJ** without added mass, and about **640 MJ** with 5 % surge added mass.
- 4 × 5,509 kips ≈ 98 MN, so NTSB's AASHTO force was above about 98 MN. With DWT ≈ 116,851 t (from memory, unverified), P_s = 8.15·10.8 ft/s·√116,851 ≈ 30,100 kips ≈ 134 MN. That is 5.5× capacity, consistent with ">4×" and "nearly 5×".
- Pedersen/EN Annex C with L_pp ≈ 286 m (assumed) gives **about 320 MN** and about 3.1 m crush, roughly 2.4× AASHTO. This matches the "about twice AASHTO" statement. Use Dali as a calibration bracket for the ROM's rigid-target bow-force module (about 100–320 MN for about 0.6 GJ).
- Validation data for floating-bridge global response must come from **model tests and the Bergsøysund monitoring** (ambient/ship-wave response, modal ID), not from collision incidents. No instrumented floating-bridge ship collision was found.

### Gaps
- The exact NTSB force figure, the angle of impact and any ERDC/FHWA FE analysis of Pier 17 were not retrieved.
- No other allisions with published force or energy back-calculations were retrieved: Sunshine Skyway 1980 (mentioned only via a CNN headline), Tasman 1975, Kafjord, Tjörn 1980, and others.
- No records were found of ship strikes on Norwegian floating bridges, Hood Canal or the Lake Washington bridges. Norwegian-language searches ("påkjørsel", "kollisjon") were not run because the search budget ran out.

## 7. Rough numbers: cruise ships calling Norwegian fjords (Excel/Arvia-class, ~184k GT) at 6–12 kn

### Takeaway
Excel/Helios-class ships (AIDAnova, Iona, Arvia; about 184k GT, 337–345 m LOA, 42 m beam, about 8.8 m draught) have a listed displacement of about 80,000–87,300 t. Head-on kinetic energy is about **0.4 GJ at 6 kn, 0.75–0.8 GJ at 8 kn, 1.2 GJ at 10 kn and 1.75 GJ at 12 kn**. Rigid-wall bow forces are about **270–550 MN** (Pedersen formula). That is 2–7× the Bjørnafjorden 250 MJ girder benchmark and up to 70× the Nordhordland 25 MJ pontoon reassessment.

### Cited Findings
- **AIDAnova:** displacement **87,306 t**, LOA **337 m**, beam **42 m**, draught **8.8 m** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDAnova).
- **Iona:** displacement **87,306 t**, LOA about **344–345 m**, beam **42 m**; draught 8.8 m in one source, **11.8–12 m** in another (outlier) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS_Iona); [Cruise Hive](https://www.cruisehive.com/po-cruise-ships-by-size).
- **Arvia:** displacement **87,276 t**, LOA about **344.5 m**, beam 42 m, draught 8.5–8.8 m — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS_Arvia).
- The class article gives about **80,000 t**. Naming is inconsistent (P&O "Excel class"; class article "Excellence class", with AIDAnova in the "Helios" subclass). — [Wikipedia, Excellence-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excellence-class_cruise_ship).
- Taller modern cruise ships raise the frequency of forecastle and deckhouse collisions with bridge girders — [Sha et al. forecastle paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333867257_Design_of_steel_bridge_girders_against_ship_forecastle_collisions).

- **Kinetic-energy table (my calculation; E = ½·Δ·(1+mₐ)·V², 1 kn = 0.5144 m/s)**:

- **Rigid-wall bow force and crush** (Pedersen/EN Annex C, L_pp assumed 300–320 m): 6 kn → about 272–281 MN, s ≈ 2.4–2.5 m. 8 kn → about 363–374 MN, about 3.3 m. 10 kn → about 453–468 MN, about 4.1–4.2 m. 12 kn → about 544–562 MN, about 4.9–5.0 m.
- **Energy split on a compliant floating bridge**, using the K12 ratios as a first guess: head-on pontoon about 40 % local, so about 170–700 MJ at 6–12 kn. At 80° offset, about 80 % local, so about 350–1,400 MJ. Even 6 kn head-on exceeds the "up to 200 MJ local" K12 worst case. For a Nordfjord crossing, this suggests either (a) deliberately designed sacrificial/fendered pontoons near the channel, (b) a strict speed regime plus escort tugs, or (c) siting the channel span so cruise ships can't reach pontoons at speed.

- **Air draft vs. clearance:** if cruise ship air draft exceeds the high-span clearance, a superstructure–girder strike becomes possible. Neither value was verified here, so this needs a project-specific check.

### Gaps
- The displacement values come from Wikipedia infoboxes, and the identical 87,306 t for two ships suggests copied data. Lightship and design-draught displacement for Excel-class ships should be confirmed with the class society or yard (Meyer Werft). L_pp was not found, so 300–320 m is assumed.
- No published cruise-ship bow or superstructure F–δ curves were found; Pedersen's 1993 formula was fitted to merchant ships.
- No data on actual cruise transit speeds in Nordfjord/Norwegian fjords near bridges, from AIS or pilotage rules, was gathered in this scope.
